registration that does not exist.

## What you may do without asking

- **Use the software**, under the Apache-2.0 licence, including commercially.
- **Say what your software does** — "works with 41Prompts", "a plugin for 41Prompts",
  "compatible with the 41Prompts build format". Nominative use is the point of a name.
- **Write about the project**, favourably or otherwise, and use the name to refer to it.
- **Fork the code.** Apache-2.0 says you may, and nothing here narrows it.

## What we ask you not to do

- **Name your fork or your product after ours**, or use a name confusingly similar to it, in a way
  that suggests we published it or endorsed it.
- **Publish a package under a name that reads as ours** — anything in the `@41prompts` scope, or a
  name a reasonable person would take for an official distribution.
- **Use the logo mark as your own**, or modify it and present the result as ours.
- **Suggest an affiliation, endorsement or sponsorship** that does not exist.

The line is confusion, not use. If your users could reasonably think a thing came from us and it did
not, that is the problem this page is about.

Ask: **hello@41prompts.ai**. A plain description of what you want to do gets a plain answer, and we
would much rather answer the question than find out later.
